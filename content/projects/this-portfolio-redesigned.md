I started building my portfolio around six years ago. Back then, I had no clear idea of what I wanted it to become. I just knew I wanted to create something of my own, something different, something I could be proud of.

<img src="/media/2026/09d96a0615474c4d877ab4cb-563x762.webp" alt="Wireframe sketch of portfolio website page layout with home, projects and collaboration sections" title="Sketch of v1 Portfolio Design" width="563" height="762">

Over the years, this website went through many versions and redesigns. Every version was an attempt to get a little closer to what I had in mind. I experimented with different styles, explored new ideas, and looked at the work of designers and developers who were far better than me. I wanted to learn from them, and in many ways, I did.

But somewhere along the way, I started caring too much about how my work compared to everyone else's.

When I was starting out, I wanted to put everything in one place. Every skill, every project, every little thing I had done. I thought that if I could show enough, people would see what I was capable of. I wanted the website to stand out. I wanted to be different.

There was nothing wrong with that ambition. I was simply young, curious, and trying to figure things out.

As I spent more time learning from talented people, I began to notice something about myself. ***I was so focused on following the paths of people I admired that I had slowly forgotten to ask what I wanted for myself.*** I was learning how to make things look good, but I wasn't always thinking about whether they felt right to me.

It took time to understand that inspiration is a good place to start, but it doesn't have to be the place where you stay.

You don't need to be great when you begin. You don't need to have everything figured out, and you don't need to know exactly where you're going. *Sometimes, you just need to start creating. Make mistakes. Try again. Build something, learn from it, and keep moving.*

You learn things along the way that you could never have understood by simply waiting until you felt ready.

Over time, I also started to understand myself a little better. I discovered more of what I enjoy, what matters to me, and what kind of work gives me a sense of purpose. I found work that I care about, and I became more comfortable with the person I was becoming.

I learned that being different doesn't always mean doing something nobody has ever done before. Sometimes, it means having the courage to do things in your own way. To make decisions that reflect who you are, even when they don't follow what everyone else is doing.

You can admire someone without becoming them. You can learn from others without losing yourself in the process. And you can change your mind without treating everything you did before as a mistake.

<img src="/media/2026/366d191483f1469e9c2d19f8-827x741.webp" alt="A large, detailed mood board with various images, text, and colored rectangles on a white background" title="Entire moodboard of v1" width="827" height="741">

Looking back, I'm grateful for every version of this portfolio, even the ones I no longer like. Each one taught me something. Some taught me what I wanted to create, while others helped me understand what I didn't want. None of that time was wasted. I was learning, even when I didn't realize it.

And now, after years of experimenting and starting over, I find myself drawn to something simpler.

Something minimal, clean, and easy to understand. Something that gives each project room to speak for itself without trying to say everything at once. Something informative without being overwhelming, and simple without feeling empty.

Most importantly, something that feels like me.

I genuinely love how it turned out. Not because it's perfect, or because I believe I've finally figured everything out, but because I can look at it and recognize my own decisions in it. It no longer feels like an attempt to become someone else. It feels like a reflection of where I am today.

I'm sure there will be more changes in the future. I'll learn new things, discover new interests, and probably look back at this version one day with a different perspective. That's part of growing, and I'm learning to be okay with that.

For now, I'm happy with where I've arrived.

Six years ago, I started with the simple desire to create something I could be proud of. Today, that desire is still here. The difference is that I've learned I don't have to make something extraordinary just to prove that I'm capable of doing something meaningful.

Sometimes, it's enough to make something honest. Something thoughtful. Something that is yours.

And maybe that's what this redesign has really been about all along. Not just building a better portfolio, but learning to trust my own direction, accept where I am, and keep creating without needing to have everything figured out.

I'm still a work in progress, just like this website.

And for the first time in a long time, that feels like a good place to be.
